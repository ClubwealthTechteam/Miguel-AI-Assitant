01Purpose

The Workflow Process Form captures how one of your regular processes is done today: the tools you use, every step, where it slows down, and where you make decisions. The AI Tech team uses your answers to decide what AI and automation can take off your plate, while keeping you in control of the decisions that need a person.

Who fills it out: admins and team members across Clubwealth, Homexa and PFL who were sent the form link.

How many tasks: you can document several tasks in one form. In Q2 and Q7, click + Add another task to give each task its own process map, and in Q3–Q6 click + Add another task to add a row for each one. Use the same task title throughout so the answers line up; titles you enter in Q2 are suggested automatically in Q3–Q6.

02Before you start

  • Use your computer. The form doesn't open on phones or tablets because the process map needs a large screen and a mouse.
  • Pick the tasks to document: the ones you do often, by hand, and that take real time, for example "New client welcome posts" or "Applicant screening". You can include several in the same form.
  • Walk through it once in your head (or for real) and note each tool you touch, in order.
  • Have rough numbers ready: how often it happens (daily, weekly) and about how long each part takes.
  • Block 20–30 minutes. Your answers save in the browser as you type, so you can close the tab and come back later on the same computer and browser.

03Filling out the form, section by section

  1. Your details. Enter your full name and your CW / HX / PFL work email. The date fills in automatically.

  2. Q1. Tools, apps and spreadsheets. List every tool used in this process, one per box. Click + Add another tool for more.

    • Web tools: anything in a browser (Infusionsoft/Keap, Recruit CRM, Canva, Meta Business Suite).
    • Apps: installed or chat apps (Slack, Zoom, Messenger).
    • Spreadsheets: name the actual sheet, e.g. "Posting Calendar", not just "Google Sheets".

  3. Q2. Walk us through the process (the manual way). First, describe the whole process in your own words in the text box: what starts it, what you do, and what "done" looks like. Then build the process map below it. See Using the process map.

  4. Q3. Information you need. For each task, list the information you need and where it comes from, and mark it Required or Optional. Task titles you typed in Q2 are suggested automatically.

  5. Q4. Manual and repetitive steps. List the steps you do by hand, how often they happen, and roughly how long they take. These numbers help us choose what to automate first.

  6. Q5. Judgment and approvals. List every point where you or someone else has to decide or sign off, what that decision is based on, and whether AI could prepare it for you to review.

  7. Q6. Bottlenecks and workarounds. What slows the process down, what you currently do to get around it, and the impact (delays, rework, missed items).

  8. Q7. The ideal automated version. Describe how the process would run if it ran itself, and where you would still want to review or approve. Map it the same way as Q2. It's fine to name tools you don't use yet, or to write "automatically" where you don't know the tool.

  9. Q8. AI Task Hub preference. Click View comparison to read the pros, neutral points and cons of the Software App and the Club Wealth University webpage. Choose the one that suits your own workflow and explain why.

    This is your individual opinion. Please don't share, compare or discuss your choice with other team members before submitting.

  10. Acknowledge. Type ACKNOWLEDGED to confirm you'll be invited to the Room 10 Zoom follow-up, then click Submit form.

04Using the process map (Q2 and Q7)

The map shows your process as boxes from left to right. Each step is two boxes: the Tool on top and the Task description underneath.

Add another stepThe large plus at the end of a row adds the next step in that row.
Add a sub step: YES / NOThe small plus above (YES) or below (NO) splits the process at a decision into two paths.
DecisionOnce a step splits, a Decision box appears under it. Type the yes/no question, e.g. "Qualified?"
Add YES / NO pathIf you've only added one side of a split, the other side shows a button to add it.
Remove a stepHover over a step and click the small ✕ in its corner. Removing the last step of a YES/NO path removes that path.
Move aroundClick and drag the dotted background to pan, use Full screen for big maps, or drag the canvas's bottom-right corner to make it taller.

How to map a process

  1. Give the task a title, e.g. "New client welcome posts".
  2. Fill in the first step: the tool, and what you do in it.
  3. Click Add another step for each next action, in order.
  4. When the process depends on an answer (photo found? approved? replied?), use the YES plus above and the NO plus below. Then type the question in the Decision box.
  5. Continue each path until it ends. Paths can split again.
  6. To document another task, click + Add another task below the map. Each task gets its own map and title.

Rule of thumb: one box = one action in one tool. If you switch tools, start a new box.

05Being specific

Specific answers are what make automation possible. Vague answers lead to follow-up questions.

Too vagueSpecific (what we need)
I update the CRM.I copy the applicant's phone number from the Jotform email into Recruit CRM and change their stage to Screening.
I make the post.I duplicate the welcome template in Canva and drop in the client's photo, name and city.
It takes a while.About 15 minutes per client, 8–12 clients a week.
Approval is slow.Approval sits in Slack for about a day each round; I ping the manager again at end of day.

Do

  • Name the exact tool, sheet or saved search.
  • Describe what you copy, check, decide or send.
  • Include how often and how long.
  • Map the real process, including the messy parts and workarounds.

Don't

  • Combine several tools in one box.
  • Leave the map with only one step.
  • Describe the ideal version in Q2. Save that for Q7.
  • Paste passwords, client payment details or other sensitive data.

06Submitting and what happens next

  1. Click Submit form. If anything required is missing, it's highlighted in red and the form scrolls to it.
  2. You'll see "Thank you, your form is in." with a reference number like CW-20261006-CB80F7. Keep it in case you need to refer to your submission.
  3. Your process maps are saved as images automatically. You don't need to screenshot anything.
  4. Expect an invite to Room 10 on the CW Helpdesk Zoom with Miguel and Camille. Be ready to show the process on screen; Miguel may sit in on a shadow session.
  5. Need to change or add something after submitting? Click Edit my response on the thank-you screen. Your answers reopen in the form; make your changes and submit again. We'll use your latest version.

07Troubleshooting

What you seeWhat to do

08Worked example: New client welcome posts (Marketing)

This is a complete, realistic submission for one marketing process. Use it as a model for the level of detail we're looking for.

Q1 · Tools
  • Web tools: Facebook (Page + Groups), Meta Business Suite, Canva, Infusionsoft (Keap)
  • Apps: Slack, Messenger
  • Spreadsheets: Posting Calendar, New Client Tracker
Q2 · Walkthrough (the manual way)

Every Monday I pull last week's new clients from Infusionsoft and add them to the Posting Calendar. For each client I look for a headshot on Facebook. If there is one, I build the welcome graphic in Canva, write the caption in Google Docs and send both to the marketing manager in Slack; once approved I schedule it in Meta Business Suite for the Page and the Group. If there's no photo I message the client on Messenger and wait up to 2 days; if they don't reply I use the no-photo template. About 8–12 clients a week, roughly 25–30 minutes each.
